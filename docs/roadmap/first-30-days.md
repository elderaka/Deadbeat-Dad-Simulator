# First 30 In-Game Days — Progression Pacing Sketch

**Not a fixed campaign script, real-world delivery schedule, unlock timetable or difficulty guarantee.** This is a test plan for whether the existing household loop can meaningfully change over a month. Player choices may skip entire routes, recover early, struggle, or take alternative arrangements. Specific prices and dates are deliberately unset.

| Candidate window | Pressure and story | Opportunities | What the tester should notice |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Days 1–3: The Secret** | Inherited pinjol (optional history toggle), household pickup and basic obligations. Ibu/Anak have schedules. | Manual commute, first ojol fare, first fish sale, transparent decision to disclose or conceal. | Bapak cannot earn money or leisure without spending time; every starting difficulty has a viable honest path. |
| **Days 4–7: Finding a rhythm** | Loan/bill due; small shocks or negotiation options. | Paid pond versus public river, first pickup coverage arrangement, simple vehicle maintenance choice. | A paid service changes free time and cost; fishing locations are alternatives, not a straight upgrade. |
| **Days 8–14: Reliability** | Repeat bills and school weeks; consequences of prior promises. | Repair the Supra or save toward another option, explore new fishing conditions, neighbor relationships. | Reliable systems free time without erasing obligations; overdue debt can affect **specific** informed people. |
| **Days 15–21: Other lives** | Ibu may encounter career/schedule opportunity; Anak's interest or school activity may affect transport. | Discuss shift change, support or negotiate an activity, arrange safe transport/carpool, add one functional household purchase. | Family development changes daily decisions and is never bought with XP. |
| **Days 22–30: Choosing a lifestyle** | Savings, debt, family commitments and housing security have diverged. | Remain an angler, balance ojol, consider future nambak/second vehicle/house move; decide how much time to reclaim. | Players finish with different, explainable routines and a memorable household chronicle, not one required wealth milestone. |

## Cross-cutting rules

- No mandatory relapse, forced divorce, eviction or gambling tutorial. Financial trouble can escalate gradually but recovery must remain credible.
- The "Bapak belum pernah judol" history toggle remains a legitimate alternative opening.
- A river spot is only restricted when clearly authored and communicated; public river fishing has no automatic punishment.
- `Kolam pemancingan` (paying to fish) and `tambak` (raising fish for harvest) are separate systems.
- Family autonomy cannot be traded for player progress; Ibu's shift changes and Anak's interests can increase obligations as well as improve arrangements.
- One decision should modify a subsequent day, not just change a HUD label.

## Evaluation

Track play time per day; compulsory travel and pickup minutes; discretionary time after obligations; wallet and debt; fish/ojol net contribution after fuel and fees; family task coverage; follow-through on promises; resident awareness; and whether a player can tell the story of their last seven days.

Use **short targeted scenarios first**. The first economy [milestone](milestone-01-economy-and-consequences.md) should validate a two-day and seven-day consequence chain before anyone builds all thirty days of content.
