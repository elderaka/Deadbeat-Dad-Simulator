# Two-Day Core Loop Experiment

> **2026-11-11 status:** the bare interactions in this experiment now exist across the Kampung prototype (see [neighborhood log](neighborhood-phone.md)). The full two-day player-experience hypothesis—including comparable time-reclaimed accounting and a recurring household economic trade-off—has **not** been conclusively validated. The next gate is [M01 economy and consequences](milestone-01-economy-and-consequences.md); only then expand via the [30-day sketch](first-30-days.md).

## Hypothesis
A player can enjoy Bapak Simulator for the **strategic pleasure of reclaiming time** before they care about catching rare fish.

## Starting setup
One family (Bapak, working spouse, school-age child), rental home, basic vehicle, cash, a few bills, a short road, a factory, a school and a fishing pond. No multiplayer.

## Day 1 — Responsibility has a cost
Morning: drive child and spouse to destinations. Day: accomplish one errand, try fishing. Evening: collect family, see obligations and time spent. Show an affordable, nontrivial alternative for tomorrow's school run.

## Day 2 — Earned convenience
Player chooses to pay for school transport or coordinate a favor; it removes one repeat trip and introduces a monetary/social obligation. The freed time can be used for fishing, another task, or a family interaction. Evening: clearly display time gained, remaining obligations and family consequences.

## Acceptance questions
1. Can the tester explain why the upgrade helps without a tutorial?
2. Does the new freedom feel tangible?
3. Is there a meaningful trade-off rather than an automatic best choice?
4. Does the family seem to respond to the day?
5. Would the player replay to try a different solution?

## Risks
If transport is mere unskippable travel, the first day is tedious. If upgrades are a one-click perfect solution, the second day lacks strategy. If fishing supplies all enjoyment, the central design fails.

## Exclusions
Generational simulation, career trees, large map, advanced driving physics, production economy, online servers, localization and store deployment.

## Deliverables after approval
One-page event script for each day, responsibility data schema, basic clock/state transitions and a developer-visible time-usage report.
