# 07 — Progression, Rewards and Endgame

> **Status: PROPOSED** — Canonical = approved direction; Proposed = hypothesis to validate; Open = undecided. Numeric examples are not final balancing.

## Reward layers
- **Minute:** catch, bargain, joke, discovered neighbor interaction
- **Day:** solved obligation, extra time, surprising family exchange
- **Week:** debt reduced, upgraded furniture/vehicle, stable routine
- **Season:** promotion, school event, neighborhood milestone
- **Long term:** improved property, independent children, financial security and chosen leisure

## Suggested progression chapters (not rigid level gates)
1. **Bapak Kontrakan:** starter household, unreliable routine
2. **Bapak Mapan:** stable finances, a few durable conveniences
3. **Bapak Beranak Banyak:** *optional branch* into larger-family chaos
4. **Bapak Pengusaha:** income-producing ventures and delegation
5. **Bapak Legendaris:** a thriving household and freely chosen time

These aren't required family-size milestones. A one-child household is a valid success path.

## Player-facing measures
- Daily free-time gained (without neglected responsibilities)
- Household stability
- Responsibilities covered
- Relationship stories, not solely affection points
- Tangible upgrades and unlocks

## Chronicle
A proposed family album records meaningful events (first catch together, house purchase, graduation, work promotion). Strong retention comes from *remembering what happened in this save* rather than completing a universal checklist.

## Endgame design question
Full automation risks boredom. Preserve voluntary goals: festivals, family trips, rare fishing challenges, neighbor projects, improving the neighborhood and self-directed hobbies. **Optimize away obligation, not opportunity.**

## Victory
Not maximum money, most children or legendary fish. Success is household wellbeing plus meaningful freedom of choice. The ending could be Bapak fishing peacefully while everyone is doing well, but sandbox play continues.
