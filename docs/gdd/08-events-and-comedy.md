# 08 — Events, Consequences and Comedy

> **Status: PROPOSED** — Canonical = approved direction; Proposed = hypothesis to validate; Open = undecided. Numeric examples are not final balancing.

## Comedy source: system collisions
The funniest moments arise when ordinary Indonesian obligations collide with a leisure obsession. Rather than 500 random popups, compose a smaller event library whose triggers depend on schedules, finances, player history, relationships and location.

## Event schema (candidate)
Event ID; prerequisites; trigger window; participants; conflict; 2–4 choices; direct costs; delayed consequences; cooldown; dialogue variation; resolution state.

## Sample events (all proposals)
- **Pak, ini sudah jam berapa?** Wife calls during a difficult catch; time and reliability compete.
- **Ambil rapor:** A school event overlaps a fishing contest.
- **Rapat RT mendadak:** Neighborhood obligations interrupt plans.
- **Tanggal tua:** Bills, groceries and vehicle payments cluster.
- **Pak Ujang's angkot:** A favor may unlock school transport.
- **Strike! Lah, sandal:** A strange catch becomes a souvenir.
- **Anak ingin masuk universitas:** A child's goal changes the household savings plan.

## Social vulnerability and opportunistic offers
Certain neighbors may learn of Bapak's financial distress through events, overheard information or visible asset sales, then propose buying his belongings well below market value. Other neighbors may act fairly or supportively. The hidden financial-pressure / awareness mechanic and player agency are described in [Risk Economy §15](15-risk-economy-and-neighbor-exploitation.md) and [ADR-0006](../decisions/ADR-0006-risk-economy-social-exploitation.md). Don't make NPCs magically know private transactions or force the player to accept an offer.

## Fairness
Events must be forecastable enough to support strategy. High-stakes surprises need warnings or recovery paths. Let players learn why an event occurred. Use callback events sparingly to make choices feel remembered.

## Cultural tone
Local Indonesian humor, factory shifts, warung, RT, angkot, fishing pond, cicilan, kopi sachet; comedic subjects are situational conflicts and the fictional protagonist's rationalizations. Avoid discriminatory ethnic stereotypes or humiliation of low-wage workers.

## Writing style
Short, characterful, quotable. Do not force a scolding wife stereotype; she should have independent motivations, wit and agency.

**Open:** Storytelling voice, text-language strategy (Indonesian/English?), age rating and event density.
