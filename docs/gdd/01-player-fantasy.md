# 01 — Player Fantasy and Hook

> **Status: CANONICAL** — Canonical = approved direction; Proposed = hypothesis to validate; Open = undecided. Numeric examples are not final balancing.

## Player promise
**"I want to be useless, but I want my family to thrive."** The paradox sells the premise. Bapak loves leisure and aims for a stable, supportive household without a traditional 9-to-5 job.

## What is actually addictive?
- **Competence:** turn an annoying recurring task into a sustainable system.
- **Ownership:** a larger share of the day becomes discretionary.
- **Curiosity:** find out what the spouse, children and neighbors will do next.
- **Visible transformation:** start in a cramped rental, gradually improve the household and environment.
- **Emergent stories:** unexpected combinations of family schedules, debts, opportunities and social favors.

## Player-created story
A player earns a neighbor's favor by helping fix an angkot → negotiates a school pickup arrangement → gains time to fish → profits from a competition → starts a small fish business → buys a family upgrade. The value is the causal chain, not merely a larger currency number.

## Negative examples
- Merely increasing fishing loot rarity while the family remains static.
- Maximizing leisure by ignoring or abandoning dependent family members.
- A relationship bar that can be filled by repeating gifts.
- Automation that removes all meaningful decisions and leaves a blank endgame.

## Successful end state
The family is doing well, mundane obligations are adequately covered, and Bapak chooses what to do with his reclaimed time—often sitting by the pond drinking coffee.
